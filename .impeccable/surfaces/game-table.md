# Game Table Surface Contract

## THESIS

The table itself is the interface. It rejects the previous dashboard of trays, ledgers, cards-within-cards, and status chrome; player position, card position, scale, and motion carry the state.

## OWN-WORLD

Codex paper chrome surrounds one uninterrupted deep-ink playing surface. Ivory cards, black and vermilion suits, quiet seat names, and one amber turn/selection signal are the complete vocabulary. No casino ornament and no administrative panels.

## STORY

The player sees the two opponents across the table, reads each opponent’s latest cards directly beneath their seat, sees the current winning play at the table center, scans a large fan of their own cards, then acts from the compact control dock.

## FIRST VIEWPORT

A 48px utility bar sits above a nearly full-viewport table. AI seats float at the upper corners with count stacks; their latest face-up plays sit in the open field below them. The current winning play is large and centered. The hero’s curved hand dominates the lower edge. Status rests bottom-left and legal actions bottom-right without a separate rail.

## FORM

Pinned replacement direction approved by the user after rejecting the Action Ledger: authentic three-seat tabletop, central trick, seat-anchored plays, fan hand, minimal chrome. Evidence seed: `user-approved-tabletop-rebuild-20260817`.

## QUALITY BAR

At a glance, a player must answer: who is landlord, who just played, exactly which cards each seat last played, which play currently leads, and which actions are legal. Cards must read as cards before they read as UI controls. One throw-to-table motion establishes cause and effect; reduced motion keeps the final state.
